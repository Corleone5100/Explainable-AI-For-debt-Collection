  KEY INSIGHT: The rule-based policy achieves 46% higher per-decision reward
  than the PPO agent. This means:
    a) The PPO agent hasn't surpassed a well-tuned heuristic in efficiency
    b) The PPO agent's advantage is in handling edge cases and nuanced
       borrower profiles that rules can't capture (e.g., GAT embeddings
       revealing hidden risk)
    c) The reward function may need tuning to push the PPO past the rule-based
       ceiling

================================================================================

CRITICAL FINDINGS:

  A) GRAPH FEATURES ARE ESSENTIAL
     Baseline (8 core features only): -Rs. 15.05M (disastrous)
     +all_graph (16 features with GNN): +Rs. 1.74M (profitable)
     Difference: Rs. 16.79M improvement

     This PROVES that graph-derived features (GAT embeddings, community
     risk, multi-hop signals) are critical for the agent to make any
     profitable decisions at all.

  B) COMMUNITY FEATURES ARE THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT ADDITION
     +community alone: +Rs. 0.99M (first profitable config)
     This suggests that knowing "my borrower is in a 60% high-risk community"
     is more valuable than knowing individual structural features.

  C) STRUCTURAL FEATURES (degree, pagerank, betweenness) HURT PERFORMANCE
     baseline: -Rs. 15.05M
     +structural: -Rs. 15.77M (worse)
     Adding node centrality metrics confuses the agent. These features may
     correlate with risk in ways that mislead the policy during short training.

  D) MULTI-HOP SIGNALS ALSO HURT
     +multihop: -Rs. 16.56M (worst non-full config)
     The 2-hop and 3-hop neighborhood signals add noise rather than signal.
     The 1-hop signal alone may be sufficient.

  E) THE FULL 150-FEATURE MODEL FAILS
     full (all): -Rs. 15.43M (catastrophic, similar to baseline)
     With 150 features (including 16 GAT embeddings across multiple dimensions),
     the agent suffers from the curse of dimensionality. With only 50,000
     training timesteps, 150 features means ~333 samples per feature —
     far too few for reliable learning.

  RECOMMENDATION: The optimal feature set is +all_graph (16 features) or
  +community (10 features). Avoid the full 150-dimensional set unless
  training timesteps are increased 10x.

8. WEAKNESSES AND RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

  ISSUE 1: OVER-ESCALATION ON LOW RISK (CRITICAL)
    Problem: 24% Legal Notice on Low risk borrowers (should be 0-5%)
    Impact: -Rs. 1.3M loss on just 339 Low risk decisions
    Fix: Increase penalty for Legal on Low risk from 0.35 to 0.6+ in
         debt_env.py reward function

  ISSUE 2: UNDER-UTILIZATION OF NO ACTION ON VERY LOW RISK
    Problem: Only 34% No Action on Very Low risk (should be 50%+)
    Impact: -Rs. 297K loss from unnecessary SMS costs
    Fix: Increase reward for No Action on Very Low risk borrowers who
         have 0 overdue months

  ISSUE 3: MEDIUM RISK TREATED LIKE HIGH RISK
    Problem: 29% Legal on Medium risk (should be 20%)
    Impact: 58.4% correctness (barely above random for 4 actions = 50%)
    Fix: Add a "warning" reward tier for Medium risk that penalizes
         Legal unless overdue_months > 5

  ISSUE 4: CURSE OF DIMENSIONALITY WITH FULL FEATURE SET
    Problem: 150 features with 50K timesteps = 333 samples/feature
    Impact: Full model performs as badly as baseline (-Rs. 15.4M)
    Fix: Use +all_graph (16 features) for production. If full features
         are needed, increase training to 500K+ timesteps

  ISSUE 5: PPO DOESN'T SURPASS RULE-BASED IN EFFICIENCY
    Problem: Rule-based achieves Rs. 3,671/decision vs PPO's Rs. 2,513
    Impact: PPO is 46% less efficient per decision
    Fix: Consider hybrid approach — use rule-based for Low/Medium risk
         (where rules work well) and PPO for High/Very High (where PPO
         adds nuance through graph features)
